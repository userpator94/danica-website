# Website Development Brief — Danica Filipović

**Document type:** Product, content, UX/UI, and implementation brief  
**Project:** Personal website for student guidance and relocation support in Slovenia  
**Status:** Consolidated concept for design and development  
**Primary conversion:** Consultation inquiry / booking  
**Languages:** B/H/S (shared regional version), English, Slovenian

---

## 1. Project Overview

Build a clear, trustworthy, modern personal-brand website for **Danica Filipović**, who helps prospective international students from the Western Balkans and Bulgaria understand and navigate studying in Slovenia or participating in an Erasmus+ exchange there.

The website must explain who Danica is, why her experience is relevant, what she can help with, how the service works, and how visitors can engage at different levels of support.

This is a personal service, not a generic education agency. The central value proposition is that Danica has personally experienced the challenges of studying abroad and has subsequently supported other international students in an ambassador and tutor capacity.

The site should initially be relatively small and straightforward. It should not require a blog, client portal, complex booking system, or integrated payment system for launch. The architecture should allow these to be added later if demand justifies them.

### Primary objectives

1. Generate qualified inquiries and consultation bookings.
2. Explain the difference between degree-study support and Erasmus+ support.
3. Make the service options and boundaries understandable.
4. Establish trust through Danica’s personal story and relevant experience.
5. Serve visitors in three language versions.
6. Provide a foundation for organic search visibility and future growth.

### Success indicators

Track:
- Visits to service pages.
- Clicks on consultation and contact calls to action.
- Form starts and successful submissions.
- Inquiries by language and service interest.
- Consultation inquiries that convert into paid work.
- Most frequently requested services and packages.

No numeric targets should be invented before baseline traffic and conversion data are available.

## 2. Target Audience

### Geographic audience

The intended audience is students from:
- Serbia
- Croatia
- Bosnia and Herzegovina
- Montenegro
- North Macedonia
- Bulgaria

The site may also be relevant to students from other countries who want to study in Slovenia or attend an Erasmus+ exchange there. English content should support this broader audience without diluting the primary regional focus.

### Audience segment A — Degree students

People considering a full degree programme at a Slovenian university, including bachelor’s or master’s studies.

Typical needs:
- Understanding the Slovenian higher-education system.
- Exploring universities and study programmes.
- Assessing which programmes may fit their interests and goals.
- Understanding admission requirements, deadlines, and documentation.
- Preparing applications.
- Understanding relevant visa or residence-permit procedures, where applicable.
- Finding accommodation and preparing for the move.

### Audience segment B — Erasmus+ exchange students

Students who are already enrolled at a university elsewhere and want to spend a semester or another exchange period in Slovenia.

Typical needs:
- Understanding the host-university process.
- Preparing for the exchange and related documents.
- Understanding administrative steps and deadlines.
- Finding accommodation.
- Planning practical arrangements for arrival and student life.

This audience must be visible on the homepage as a distinct route, not treated merely as a subcategory of full-degree applicants.

### Audience segment C — Students preparing to relocate

Students who have already selected a programme or received confirmation but need practical help with the remaining steps.

Typical needs:
- Reviewing documents and application materials.
- Help completing forms and applications.
- Guidance on relevant administrative procedures.
- Housing search support.
- A practical relocation checklist and structured support before departure.

### Audience needs and concerns

Visitors may feel overwhelmed by unfamiliar requirements, unclear processes, deadlines, housing uncertainty, and the prospect of moving without an established support network. The website should reduce uncertainty by explaining the process clearly and making the next action obvious.

Do not assume every visitor needs the most comprehensive package. The site must show that a single consultation is a valid starting point.

## 5. Services and Commercial Model

The commercial model is **hybrid**:
1. A one-off consultation.
2. Defined support packages.
3. Additional individual services priced separately where needed.

Do not present the packages as finalized until Danica has confirmed exact deliverables, time commitments, prices, availability, and service boundaries.

### Service 1 — Initial Consultation

**For:** Students who are exploring options or need clarity on a particular question.

Potential scope:
- Discussing the student’s goals and current situation.
- General information about studying and living in Slovenia.
- A high-level overview of the enrollment or Erasmus+ process.
- Initial guidance on relevant requirements and options.
- Agreeing on practical next steps.

**Outcome:** The student has a clearer understanding of the situation and what to do next.

This should be available as a standalone paid service.

### Service 2 — Study Planning and Application Support

**For:** Students who need help selecting a programme and understanding how to apply.

Potential scope:
- Exploring relevant universities and study programmes.
- Discussing academic interests and goals.
- Understanding admission criteria, deadlines, and required documents.
- Reviewing application materials and documents.
- Guidance with completing application forms.
- Planning the sequence of application steps.

**Outcome:** A clearer academic choice and a more organized application process.

Do not imply that Danica can guarantee admission or make admissions decisions.

### Service 3 — Application and Administrative Preparation

**For:** Students who need help with university applications and preparation for relevant visa or residence-permit procedures.

Potential scope:
- The relevant academic and application guidance above.
- Reviewing documents for completeness and consistency.
- Help understanding and completing forms.
- Explaining administrative steps and deadlines.
- Helping the student identify official sources and requirements applicable to their situation.
- A checklist of tasks and documents to prepare.

**Outcome:** Better-organized preparation for the relevant application and administrative processes.

Visa and residence-permit support must be described carefully. Unless Danica is qualified and authorized to provide regulated immigration advice or representation, position this as guidance on publicly available requirements, document preparation, and process navigation—not legal representation or a guarantee of approval.

### Service 4 — Accommodation Search Support

**For:** Students who need help finding a place to live in Slovenia.

Potential scope:
- Explaining available accommodation routes and common considerations.
- Helping the student define requirements, budget, location, and timing.
- Identifying and comparing potential housing options.
- Guidance on the practical steps of contacting providers or landlords.

The exact level of hands-on search, communication with providers, and document assistance must be agreed in advance. Do not imply that accommodation is guaranteed.

### Service 5 — Full Student Support / Relocation Preparation

**For:** Students who want structured support across several stages of the process.

Potential scope:
- Study-programme exploration and enrollment guidance.
- Application and document review.
- Help completing relevant forms.
- Guidance on administrative and visa/residence-permit preparation.
- Accommodation-search support.
- A personalized pre-departure and relocation checklist.
- Agreed follow-up support before departure.

**Outcome:** A coordinated plan for the student’s academic, administrative, and accommodation preparation.

Define what “support until relocation” means in practical terms: duration, number of calls, communication channels, response times, included tasks, and exclusions. Avoid implying unlimited availability.

### Erasmus+ support path

Erasmus+ should be a distinct entry point on the site. It may share some services with degree applicants, but the process and needs are different.

Potential support:
- Understanding the host-university process.
- Planning the steps and deadlines.
- Reviewing relevant documents and forms.
- Accommodation search guidance.
- Preparing practical arrangements for the exchange.

Confirm which parts of the Erasmus+ process Danica can support based on her experience and the student’s specific circumstances.

### Recommended presentation of packages

Present four progressive support levels as a starting concept:

| Level | Working name | Main purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Initial Consultation | Answer questions and clarify next steps |
| 2 | Study Planning & Application | Programme selection and application preparation |
| 3 | Application & Relocation Preparation | Application plus administrative and relocation preparation |
| 4 | Full Student Support | Coordinated, agreed support across the main preparation stages |

Additional tasks can be purchased separately.

The final offer may use different package names. Avoid suggesting that each higher tier automatically includes every possible service; list exactly what is included.

### Pricing requirements

Before launch, Danica must decide:
- Price and duration of the initial consultation.
- Deliverables included in each package.
- Number and duration of calls, if applicable.
- Whether document review has limits on pages, files, or revisions.
- Whether application form completion is included or charged separately.
- What accommodation-search assistance includes.
- The length of the support period.
- Communication channels and expected response times.
- Additional-service rates.
- Cancellation, rescheduling, refund, and payment terms.
- Whether services are offered remotely, in person, or both.

If prices are not ready for launch, the website can invite visitors to request a consultation or ask for a tailored quote. Do not invent prices.

## 8. Language Strategy

The site should launch with three language versions:

1. **B/H/S** — a shared, neutral Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian regional version, written in Latin script initially.
2. **English** — international version.
3. **Slovenian (Slovenščina)** — local-language version.

### B/H/S content policy

Bosnian, Croatian, Serbian, and Montenegrin are closely related but have distinct standard forms and preferences. The launch version should use clear, neutral wording understandable across the region, without falsely claiming that one text is four fully localized national-language versions.

- Use Latin script for the initial shared version.
- Avoid region-specific idioms where a neutral alternative exists.
- Use terminology that is broadly understood in the region.
- Review the copy with a competent native or professional language reviewer before launch.
- If traffic or customer demand later justifies it, adapt the regional copy to individual national standards.

Do not label the language selector with a national flag. Use a clear text label such as “B/H/S” or “BCS”. Decide the public-facing label based on Danica’s preference and review it with a native-language professional.

### Suggested URL structure

Use separate, stable URLs for each language, for example:
- `/en/` — English.
- `/bhs/` — shared B/H/S version.
- `/sl/` — Slovenian.

Example page paths:
- `/en/services/`
- `/bhs/usluge/` (confirm suitable regional URL wording during localization)
- `/sl/storitve/`

The exact URL slugs can be finalized during implementation. They should be stable, readable, and consistent.

### Language-switching behavior

- Switching languages should preserve the equivalent page where a translation exists.
- If the equivalent page does not exist, send the visitor to the relevant language homepage rather than a broken URL.
- The language selector must work on desktop and mobile.
- Do not force a language redirect based solely on IP address.
- Browser-language suggestions may be offered unobtrusively but should not prevent manual choice.

### Localization and SEO

- Translate page titles, meta descriptions, navigation, form labels, validation messages, FAQ, CTAs, and legal/privacy interface text as appropriate.
- Use correct language metadata and `hreflang` annotations for actual available versions.
- Use self-referencing canonical URLs for indexable language pages, with appropriate cross-language alternate links.
- Generate language-specific XML sitemap entries.
- Do not publish incomplete or machine-translated content as if professionally reviewed.
- Avoid duplicate pages that differ only in a language label.
- B/H/S is a practical editorial grouping, not a guarantee that all search engines will treat it as a distinct standardized language locale. Validate the technical SEO implementation for the selected language tags and URL structure.

## 9. Visual Direction and Design System

### Overall direction

**Personal, trustworthy, European, student-friendly.**

The design should feel warm and modern, with a strong human presence. It should not look like a corporate agency or official university portal.

### Suggested color palette

- Deep forest green: `#263C35` — primary brand color, navigation, headings, important buttons.
- Warm ivory: `#F1EEE7` — main warm background.
- Terracotta: `#B97756` — restrained accent for links or small highlights.
- White or neutral surfaces — cards and content areas.
- Readable dark neutral text and accessible muted text colors.

These are initial design recommendations and can be adjusted after checking contrast and photography.

### Typography

- Expressive serif typeface for major headings.
- Clean sans-serif typeface for body copy, forms, navigation, and small labels.
- Use fonts with full support for Latin characters and diacritics used in all three language versions.
- Confirm rendering of characters such as č, ć, š, ž, đ, and Slovenian diacritics.

### Photography

Prioritize:
- Authentic portraits of Danica.
- University of Ljubljana and recognizable Ljubljana environments where relevant.
- Student life and international student settings.
- Accommodation or relocation context only where it adds useful meaning.

Avoid relying exclusively on generic stock photos. If authentic photos are unavailable at launch, use a restrained, coherent set of editorial-style images and replace them with Danica’s own approved photographs when possible.

### Layout and components

- Generous whitespace.
- Clear typographic hierarchy.
- Short, scannable sections.
- Rounded cards used consistently and sparingly.
- Strong contrast for calls to action.
- Simple icons supporting comprehension, not decorative clutter.
- Consistent card, button, form, and spacing systems.
- Avoid excessive animations, carousels, pop-ups, or decorative flags.

### Responsive behavior

The site must work on:
- Mobile phones.
- Tablets.
- Desktop screens.

On mobile:
- Stack the two audience paths vertically.
- Keep the main CTA visible and easy to reach.
- Use a compact navigation menu.
- Ensure language selection remains discoverable.
- Make forms easy to complete with a touch interface.
- Avoid tiny type, horizontal overflow, and overly tall image sections.

### Accessibility

- Semantic heading hierarchy.
- Keyboard-operable navigation, language selector, forms, and dialogs.
- Visible focus states.
- Accessible labels and error messages for form fields.
- Alt text for meaningful images; decorative images should not create redundant screen-reader output.
- Sufficient color contrast.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Do not communicate information by color alone.

## 12. Technical and Implementation Requirements

The exact technology stack is to be selected based on the developer’s workflow and maintenance needs. Do not prescribe a complex stack without a reason.

### General requirements

- Use a maintainable implementation and reusable page components.
- Keep all three language versions organized and easy to update.
- Ensure fast page loading and responsive images.
- Avoid unnecessary third-party scripts.
- Store form submissions securely and restrict access appropriately.
- Use environment variables for server-side secrets.
- Validate and sanitize submitted form data.
- Include useful error handling for form delivery and any external integration.
- Configure a production domain and HTTPS.
- Include a basic staging or preview workflow before production release.
- Provide Danica with simple instructions for changing text, images, prices, and contact details, or select a content-management approach appropriate to her needs.

### Analytics and privacy

Choose an analytics setup only after deciding what data is necessary. Configure consent and disclosures as required by applicable law and the selected tools. Avoid unnecessary tracking.

The website must provide a privacy notice that explains what data is collected through the form, why it is collected, who receives it, how long it is retained, and how a person can exercise applicable rights. Obtain appropriate legal review for the actual operating setup.

### Performance

- Optimize images and use modern formats where supported.
- Avoid oversized JavaScript bundles.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold imagery where appropriate.
- Reserve image dimensions to reduce layout shifts.
- Minimize render-blocking resources.
- Test mobile performance with real-world constraints.
- Aim for strong Core Web Vitals and a high PageSpeed score, but do not compromise accessibility or meaningful content to chase a score alone.

### Security and maintenance

- Keep dependencies updated.
- Protect forms against spam and common abuse.
- Use HTTPS.
- Avoid exposing credentials, API keys, or private data in client code.
- Define who is responsible for domain renewal, hosting, updates, backups, and form-delivery monitoring.

## 18. Final Product Direction

Build a small, polished, multilingual personal-brand website that makes Danica’s experience and practical support easy to understand.

The visitor should be able to answer, within a short time:
1. Is this service relevant to my situation?
2. Can Danica help with my particular next steps?
3. What kind of support can I choose?
4. How do I contact her?

The site should feel personal rather than institutional, practical rather than bureaucratic, and transparent rather than sales-heavy.

**The core promise:** Danica helps students approach studying in Slovenia or an Erasmus+ exchange with a clearer plan, practical guidance, and support tailored to their needs.
